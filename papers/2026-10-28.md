<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

For a complex wave, the local phase variation provides the instantaneous frequency in time, the local wavenumber in space, and the local momentum in quantum mechanics. Here we study this quantity from the complex-zero structure of the analytic continuation of the wave. A single nonreal zero produces a localized Lorentzian phase variation on the real axis, whose position, width, sign, amplitude, and integrated phase are fixed by the zero itself. This establishes a direct relation between local phase structure and the amplitude and phase response of the transfer function, suggesting a zero-based form of spectroscopy in which system parameters can be inferred from the motion, width, and orientation of chirpons. More broadly, the chirpon description provides a unified framework for instantaneous frequency, local wavenumber, superoscillation, backflow, and other local phase phenomena, while offering a new route for probing linear systems through the dynamics of their complex zeros.

The mathematical architecture is reference-agnostic, domain-extensible, and maintains provable lower-bound monotonicity for eta_wall and eta_DRE under spectral truncation. Open-source reference software is provided to facilitate deployment and standardized rating of broad-spectrum light sources.
