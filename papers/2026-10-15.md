- **评分**: ⭐ 88/100 | **日期**: 2026-10-13

- **标签**: `physics.optics`
- **AI摘要**: 提出相位敏感放大锁模新机制
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Mode locking is the mechanism by which lasers organize many optical modes into stable trains of ultrashort pulses. It underpins optical frequency combs, precision timing and spectroscopy, and provides the phase-coherent waveforms required for applications ranging from strong-field physics to optical frequency metrology. Conventional pulse-forming mechanisms select pulse amplitude without fixing its phase relative to an external optical reference, leaving pulse formation and optical-phase stabilization as separate functions. Phase-sensitive amplification offers a different possibility: the optical reference can participate directly in pulse formation. Here we demonstrate this principle in a continuously pumped oscillator. Kerr-induced phase evolution and cavity detuning turn pump-referenced parametric gain into a power-dependent discriminator that supports stable single-soliton operation. Roundtrip-resolved interferometry reveals that the soliton optical phase remains referenced to the master laser modulo $π$. These results establish phase-sensitive mode locking, in which pulse-amplitude selection and optical-phase referencing arise from the same coherent nonlinear feedback.

<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Coloring building-integrated photovoltaics poses a dilemma: absorptive pigments cost photocurrent, while ordered photonic structures are iridescent. Amorphous photonic glasses, disordered packings of high-index nanospheres, are proposed to avoid both, yet their angle-independent color has rarely been quantified on a realistic module stack. A non-absorbing scatterer needs a high index with visible transparency, which the Moss rule caps near 2.35. Disorder does not confer angle independence: the colors hold for an observer moving under a fixed sun but drift by DeltaE00 of 5 to 18 over the day, terracotta least, and a facade lit from above and viewed from below shifts every color towards the blue. Oxide, layer number, and facade geometry must therefore be designed together.

<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Lattice vibrations govern the propagation of heat in materials, and investigating their dynamics through time-resolved phonon dispersion measurements would provide insight into these excitations and their interactions. However, time-resolved measurements that directly resolve the phonon dispersion at the nanoscale have not been previously demonstrated. Momentum-resolved vibrational electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS) provides access to phonon energies and momenta with nanoscale spatial resolution. Here, we combine momentum-resolved vibrational EELS with pulsed laser heating and synchronised gated electron detection to observe the phonon dispersion of graphite during controlled heating and cooling on the microsecond timescale. Recording both the gain and loss spectra at defined times relative to laser excitation, we determine the temperature at each time delay and follow changes to the phonon branches as a function of temperature. The momentum-resolved spectra reveal a decrease in energy of the optical phonon branch with increasing temperature accompanied by an increase in peak width. These changes are consistent with temperature-dependent phonon renormalisation and scattering associated with anharmonic interactions. The approach is compatible with detector gating on timescales down to 50 ns, providing a route towards extending these measurements to faster dynamics. This work demonstrates time-resolved phonon dispersion spectroscopy using vibrational EELS and provides a route towards probing temperature-dependent phonon dynamics at the nanoscale with simultaneous energy and momentum resolution.
